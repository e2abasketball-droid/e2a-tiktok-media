Why do the best teams pass up a good shot? Because they are hunting a great one. This is the extra pass.
Here is the set-up. Five players spread around the three-point line. The player on the wing has the ball, and the defenders are guarding their own player.
Here is the key. When one defender leaves their player to help, someone else is open. Your job is to find that player, and move the ball before the defence can recover.
Option one. Drive and kick. The wing attacks the lane, and the defender from the top steps in to stop the ball. That leaves the top player open, so the pass goes out, and the shot is there. Coaching cue: pass early, before the helper gets set.
Option two. The extra pass. If the defender gets back in time, do not force the shot. Swing the ball again to the next teammate, whose defender has just moved toward the lane. That defender has to close out a second time, and that is when the shot is truly open. Coaching cue: catch ready, and move the ball quickly. Do not hold it.
Two things to watch. If the defender flies out too fast, attack the closeout and drive past. And if nobody helps, you do not need the extra pass. Take the shot you already have.
The best shot is rarely the first one. Pass the good shot up, to get the great one.
