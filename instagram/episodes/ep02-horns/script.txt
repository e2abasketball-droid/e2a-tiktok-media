What makes the Horns set so hard to defend? One setup. Three different ways to score.
The guard starts at the top with the ball. Two big players stand at the elbows, and the wings spread to the corners. Everyone has a job before the first dribble.
Here is the key. The guard can attack either screen, and the defence has to guess which one before it happens.
Option one. The guard uses the screen and drives. If the big man's defender stops the ball, the screener rolls to the rim. Coaching cue: hit the roller early, with a pass to the chest.
Option two. If the defence collapses to stop the roll, the corner defender helps. So kick the ball out. The shooter in the corner gets a clean look. Coaching cue: draw two defenders, then pass to the open player.
Two common variations. In the twist, the guard uses one screen, then comes back off the second. In the flare, the first screener steps out to the three-point line, using a flare screen from the other big.
Same set. Different reads. One set. Multiple threats.
