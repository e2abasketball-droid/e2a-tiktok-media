How do you stop a ball screen without switching? Meet ICE. One defence that sends the dribbler away from the middle of the floor, and into trouble.
Here is the set-up. A guard has the ball on the wing. A big player walks up to set the ball screen, and the screen is angled toward the middle. Everyone else spaces out around the three-point line.
Here is the key. Instead of fighting through the screen, the defence closes the door. The guard's defender gets on the high side, so the ball handler cannot use the screen, and is pushed toward the baseline, where there is less room to work.
Option one. The on-ball defender. Turn your feet to get above the ball, and force the dribbler to the sideline. Coaching cue: show your hips to the middle, and keep the dribbler on the baseline side.
Option two. The screener's defender. He drops back between the ball and the basket, and calls it early. Ice, ice, ice. Coaching cue: talk first, then move, so your teammate knows the plan before the screen arrives.
Two things to watch. If the screener pops out to the three-point line, the big must recover to the shooter. If the dribbler turns away from the screen, the on-ball defender keeps the same angle, and the big stays back.
Same screen. One simple plan. Close the middle, and make the offence work from the edge.
